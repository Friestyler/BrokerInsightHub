You are building a decision-support component for insurance account managers who manage large commercial clients.

The context: This panel is part of a broader Customer Portfolio View, where the account manager can already see active contracts and expiring policies. Your job is to now build the “Smart Coverage Gaps” section — where the system recommends the most relevant additional products to propose.


🎯 Purpose of This Panel

Account managers need to:
	1.	Understand which important coverages are missing from the customer’s portfolio
	2.	Know why those gaps matter
	3.	See what to offer now and how valuable it is
	4.	Act with confidence, backed by industry insight, compliance cues, and value estimates

This isn’t a product catalog. This is a focused, strategic recommendation engine.

⸻

🧩 What You Must Build

You must create a Smart Coverage Gaps panel that includes:


✅ 1. A set of 3–4 highly relevant coverage suggestions

Each shown as a card, ranked by impact and urgency.

Every card must include:
	•	Product name (e.g., Cyber Security Insurance)
	•	Contextual reason it’s relevant
	•	“Common in companies like this”
	•	“Required due to regulation”
	•	“Part of bundle customer is partially covered for”
	•	Projected premium value (e.g., +€1,200/year)
	•	Visual label for one of the following:
	•	🔵 High Priority
	•	🟢 Profile Match
	•	🟠 Seasonal
	•	🟣 Bundle Eligible

And a CTA:
	•	Create an opportunity, add to a campaign

⸻

✅ 2. Visual Consistency Across Cards

Each card should follow the same layout:
	1.	Top left: Icon (insurance or risk symbol)
	2.	Top right: Label tag (e.g., “High Priority”)
	3.	Middle: Product name and 2-line description of business logic
	4.	Bottom left: Premium uplift
	5.	Bottom right: Action button

DO NOT vary the layout across cards. This panel must feel ordered, deliberate, and polished.

⸻

✅ 3. Implicit Ranking by Relevance
	•	First card = most important opportunity
	•	Visual priority should be clear via layout or badge
	•	DO NOT display more than 4 cards
	•	If more suggestions are available, add a “Show More” button (but hide it by default)

⸻

✅ 4. Strategic Relevance Logic

These suggestions are not random. They’re based on:
	•	What the customer already has
	•	What’s common among similar customers
	•	What’s required by law, industry, or region
	•	What’s part of bundle logic but missing
	•	Risk exposure if coverage is not in place

Use insurance industry language, not marketing terms.

Examples of strong descriptions:
	•	“Regulatory compliance for companies with 10+ employees”
	•	“Typical add-on for construction businesses with mobile staff”
	•	“Required for businesses handling client data in the EU”

⸻

🎨 Design Requirements
	•	Use shadcn/ui components
	•	Style like an Apple product: clean, quiet, confident
	•	Use 4 clear colors for label badges only
	•	Keep icons subtle and aligned to insurance (risk shield, scales, document, building, suitcase)

⸻

❌ You MUST NOT:
	•	Use a generic product list
	•	Show more than 4 cards by default
	•	Include coverage the customer already has
	•	Use vague phrases like “You might also like”

⸻

✅ Success =

The account manager opens this panel and immediately sees:

“These are the 3–4 most important coverage additions I should propose, and here’s exactly why, what it’s worth, and how to act.”

They feel like the system has already done 80% of the thinking — and they just need to click “Create opportunity.”
