🇫🇷 French Baroque & Rococo Style (Close to Duphly)
1. Jean-Philippe Rameau (1683–1764)

2. François Couperin (1668–1733)
His Ordres (suites) have poetic titles and delicate ornamentation.

Very expressive and intimate, perfect for piano or harpsichord.

3. Claude-Bénigne Balbastre (1724–1799)
A contemporary of Duphly.

Flashier, with more galant/early classical influences, but still rooted in the French clavecin tradition.

4. Louis-Claude Daquin (1694–1772)
Known for Le Coucou and other vivid character pieces.

Lighter but charming and technically satisfying.

🎹 German Baroque with Expressive Keyboard Works
5. Carl Philipp Emanuel Bach (1714–1788)
Not exactly Baroque but crucial — bridges Baroque and Classical (Empfindsamer Stil).

Try his Solfeggietto, but more importantly, his keyboard Sonatas and Fantasias.

6. Georg Böhm (1661–1733)
Early influence on J.S. Bach.

Inventive preludes and partitas, less dense than Bach’s later works.

7. Johann Jakob Froberger (1616–1667)
Earlier than Duphly, but very introspective and poetic.

His Lamentations and Allemandes are stunning on the piano.

🇮🇹 Italian and Iberian Flavors
8. Domenico Scarlatti (1685–1757)
Explosive sonatas, full of Spanish flair, unexpected modulations, and rhythmic twists.

Works incredibly well on piano.

9. Antonio Soler (1729–1783)
Student of Scarlatti, with similar fire and virtuosity.

Some sonatas are dramatic, others almost meditative.
